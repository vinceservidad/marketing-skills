# Awareness, Belief, and Desire Map

Use this reference between verified research and angle generation when the audience's current understanding or belief is likely to determine which creative direction can be credible.

This is a framework for organizing evidence, not a claim that every purchase follows a fixed psychological sequence. Classify the map as a framework under [`KNOWLEDGE-TAXONOMY.md`](../_shared/KNOWLEDGE-TAXONOMY.md). Audience beliefs, desires, and awareness must come from supplied or traceable evidence; mark unknowns instead of inventing them.

## Mapping sequence

1. **Audience situation** — What is happening in the person's life, work, or buying context now?
2. **Pain, desire, or JTBD** — What progress are they trying to make, what friction are they escaping, or what outcome are they seeking?
3. **Awareness** — What do they already know about the problem, solution category, product, or offer?
4. **Current belief** — What do they presently believe about the problem, available solutions, this product, the effort required, the risk, or themselves?
5. **Blocking belief** — Which belief, uncertainty, objection, or missing proof can prevent the next action?
6. **Required belief shift** — What must become credible for the next action to make sense?
7. **Evidence available** — What product truth, demonstration, customer evidence, mechanism, comparison, guarantee, offer detail, or other substantiation can support that shift?
8. **Angle implication** — Which strategic reason to care can honestly connect the desired progress to the available proof?

## Awareness guidance

Use awareness as a message-fit variable, not a rigid funnel label.

- **Unaware:** Lead with a recognizable situation, tension, identity, or desired progress before assuming problem knowledge.
- **Problem-aware:** Clarify the cost, consequence, or mechanism of the problem without manufacturing urgency.
- **Solution-aware:** Differentiate solution mechanisms, trade-offs, or approaches.
- **Product-aware:** Address product-specific objections, proof, differentiation, and offer fit.
- **Most-aware:** Reduce friction around the offer, timing, value, or next action without repeating basic education unnecessarily.

These are starting hypotheses. Actual account evidence can override the default message fit.

## Belief-shift rule

A required belief shift should be stated as a testable communication job, for example:

> From: "This seems useful, but I doubt it will fit my routine."
> To: "I can see exactly how this fits into my routine with little added effort."

Do not write the desired belief first and then invent evidence to support it. If the business cannot substantiate the shift, change the angle or mark the evidence gap.

## Desire rule

Separate the desired **progress** from the product itself. "I want this product" is usually not the underlying desire. Look for the functional, emotional, social, or identity-related progress the product may help enable, while avoiding unsupported claims about what the product can deliver.

## Output

Record, at minimum:

- audience situation
- pain/desire/JTBD
- awareness
- current belief
- blocking belief
- required belief shift
- available proof
- evidence gaps
- angle implication

The map feeds angle generation. It does not replace customer research, ICP/JTBD work, claim substantiation, or controlled testing.
