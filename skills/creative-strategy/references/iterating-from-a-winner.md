# Iterating from a Winner

The skill's existing method covers building and launching an initial test matrix; this reference covers what happens after a cell wins — a distinct phase with its own discipline, not a repeat of the initial matrix-building method.

## Method

1. Confirm the win against the skill's existing standard: business outcome, conversion quality, and spend — not CTR alone — and confirm it cleared the success signal and guardrail defined before production, per the skill's existing method step 5.
2. Identify what the winning cell actually varied relative to its nearest comparison cells; a win is informative only in relation to what it beat and on which dimension it differed.
3. Hold the winning angle fixed — it is the validated variable — and vary exactly one further dimension in the next round: the same hook with a different creator or presenter, the same script with a different opening, the same offer framing with a different visual treatment, the same angle with a different proof element.
4. Define a new success signal, guardrail, and decision window for the follow-up round before producing it, the same discipline required for the original matrix per the skill's existing method.
5. Where a winning angle is being scaled across a platform or format the original test did not cover, route the platform-fit question to the owning channel skill (`$tiktok-ads`, `$youtube-ads`, or the relevant platform) rather than assuming the winning execution transfers unedited.

## Rules

- A win in one matrix cell is evidence for that angle in that scope — population, period, platform, format — not proof the angle is universally strong; treat scaling to a new scope as a new test, not an assumption.
- Do not vary more than one dimension when iterating from a winner; changing the hook, format, and offer simultaneously in the same follow-up round produces a result that cannot be attributed to any one change, defeating the purpose of iterating from a validated angle.
- A single win is not replication; per this system's general standard, require the angle to hold across at least one further comparable test before treating it as a durable pattern rather than a one-time result.
- Do not conflate "the ad won" with "the angle is proven" — a specific execution (creator, visual, exact copy) can win or lose somewhat independent of the underlying angle's strength; the iteration process is partly how the two get disentangled over successive rounds.
- Where creative-refresh cadence becomes a binding constraint on how many iteration rounds can run, route to `$optimization-scaling`'s creative-capacity gate rather than compressing the iteration discipline to fit an unstated capacity limit.
