# Creative Mechanics

Use this reference after an angle is selected and before the final concept, hook, and visual format are locked. A creative mechanic is the meaning-making structure that determines how the audience is led to understand, feel, or conclude the angle.

Classify this as a methodology and pattern catalog under [`KNOWLEDGE-TAXONOMY.md`](../_shared/KNOWLEDGE-TAXONOMY.md). A mechanic is a hypothesis about communication structure, not evidence that an ad will perform.

## Layer boundaries

- **Angle** — the strategic reason to care.
- **Creative mechanic** — how the concept causes the audience to reach or feel that reason.
- **Concept** — the specific scene, narrative, demonstration, or idea built around the mechanic.
- **Hook** — the opening expression that earns the next moment of attention.
- **Visual format** — the production vessel used to deliver the concept.
- **Proof** — the evidence supporting the claim or belief shift.

A mechanic can travel across formats. A format can carry different mechanics. Do not use either term as a synonym for concept.

## Mechanic families

Use these as starting categories, not quotas.

### Direct demonstration
The product or mechanism is shown doing the relevant job. The audience reaches the point through observable use rather than explanation alone.

Use when the product truth can be demonstrated clearly. Do not stage a result that the product cannot substantiate.

### Controlled contrast
Two relevant states, methods, or alternatives are juxtaposed so the audience can see a meaningful difference.

Use only when the comparison is fair, supportable, and not dependent on a misleading baseline.

### Reframe
The concept starts from an existing belief, assumption, or familiar explanation and introduces a more useful interpretation.

Use when the challenged belief is actually evidenced or reasonably established. A provocative contradiction with no support is not a reframe; it is an unsupported claim.

### Third-party witness
The concept makes the audience observe another person's reaction, use, recommendation, or verification rather than relying only on the brand's self-description.

Use genuine customer, expert, creator, or other third-party evidence when presented as real. Do not fabricate a testimonial, expert endorsement, or social interaction.

### Implied conclusion
The concept presents enough evidence, context, or juxtaposition for the audience to infer the point without the brand stating every conclusion directly.

Use only when the implication remains truthful. Indirect wording does not bypass claim or comparison standards.

### Discovery or conversation
The audience encounters the product or insight through a conversation, question, comment, search, message, recommendation, or discovery moment.

Use when the discovery context is plausible. Simulated conversations must not be presented as genuine customer evidence.

### Narrative reveal
A story or sequence creates tension, progression, or curiosity before revealing the product, mechanism, or insight as part of the resolution.

Use when the narrative earns the reveal and the product's role is truthful rather than artificially inserted.

### Proof stack
Several compatible proof elements build the case together: demonstration, sourced numbers, reviews, credentials, product facts, guarantees, or other substantiation.

Use when each proof element is independently supportable. More proof does not rescue a weak or overstated claim.

### Educational resolution
The concept teaches a useful mechanism, distinction, process, or category insight, then connects that learning to the product or offer.

Use when the education is accurate and useful independent of the sale. Do not manufacture a technical explanation to create false authority.

## Selection method

1. Start with the required belief shift and selected angle.
2. Identify what the audience would need to see, understand, compare, experience, or infer for the angle to become credible.
3. Select one primary mechanic that best performs that communication job.
4. Add a secondary mechanic only when it adds a distinct job rather than decorative complexity.
5. Build the concept around the mechanic.
6. Choose a hook and visual format that help the mechanic land.
7. Define the proof dependency before production.

## Rules

- Select mechanics from the evidence and communication job, not because a named pattern is fashionable.
- One mechanic can support many concepts; one concept should still have a clear primary mechanic.
- A successful execution does not prove the mechanic is universally effective.
- Do not infer a competitor, customer result, expert endorsement, scarcity condition, or product mechanism that cannot be substantiated.
- Platform-specific native fit remains owned by the relevant channel skill.
- If two proposed concepts use different surface visuals but cause the audience to reach the same conclusion in the same way, they may be execution variants rather than distinct mechanic tests.

## Output

For a selected cell, record:

- angle
- required belief shift
- primary mechanic
- optional secondary mechanic
- concept
- hook
- visual format
- proof dependency
- expected learning

The mechanic is part of concept architecture. It remains a hypothesis until tested.
