# Hook Execution

Use this reference after the angle and primary creative mechanic are selected. A hook is the opening expression of the creative idea. Its job is to earn the next moment of attention while preserving the underlying angle, proof boundary, and audience fit.

Classify this as a technique and pattern catalog under [`KNOWLEDGE-TAXONOMY.md`](../_shared/KNOWLEDGE-TAXONOMY.md). Hook patterns and psychological principles can guide execution, but they do not prove performance.

## Required inputs

- selected angle and hypothesis
- primary creative mechanic
- audience situation and pain/desire/JTBD
- best-supported awareness and belief state
- verified customer language when available
- product truth and proof limits
- visual format and placement when already known
- brand voice and compliance constraints
- test variable and success signal

If an input is unknown, keep it unknown rather than inventing customer psychology or voice.

## Hook layers

Keep these layers distinct:

- **Hook objective** — what the opening must accomplish for this audience now: recognition, curiosity, credibility, differentiation, objection handling, desire, urgency, or another specific job.
- **Tactic / frame** — the structural shape of the opening, such as question, direct problem, contrast, story, proof, explainer, callout, offer, or risk reversal.
- **Psychological mechanism** — why the frame may earn attention: recognition, curiosity, surprise, identity relevance, tension, desire, credibility, loss/stakes, or social validation.
- **Voice pattern** — the sentence-level container or conversational rhythm used to make the hook sound natural for the audience and placement.
- **Modality** — spoken line, visual first frame, text overlay, headline, caption opening, or a coordinated combination.

A tactic is not an angle. A psychological mechanism is not evidence. A voice pattern is not customer language unless it comes from a traceable customer source.

## Execution method

1. **Preserve the angle.** State the single reason to care the hook must open. Reject drafts that create a different proposition just to sound more dramatic.
2. **Set the hook objective.** Use awareness, belief state, mechanic, and destination to decide what the first moment must accomplish.
3. **Choose a fitting frame.** Select only a few frames that serve the objective rather than forcing coverage of a hook taxonomy.
4. **Select the attention mechanism.** Decide whether the opening relies primarily on recognition, curiosity, surprise, identity, desire, credibility, tension, or real stakes.
5. **Write in audience-compatible language.** Prefer traceable customer wording where available. When using synthesized language, label it as synthesis rather than VOC.
6. **Coordinate modalities.** For video, align spoken opening, first visual, and text overlay so they reinforce rather than repeat or contradict one another. For static, align headline hierarchy with the visual thesis.
7. **Generate controlled variants.** Vary one meaningful hook dimension per learning cell when practical: frame, first sentence, proof opening, visual opening, or voice register.
8. **Preflight proof and compliance.** Check that urgency, scarcity, results, guarantees, comparisons, credentials, numbers, and implied outcomes are all supportable.

## Useful hook frames

Use these as a non-exhaustive reference:

- direct problem or situation
- question
- curiosity / incomplete information
- outcome or desired progress
- contrast or mismatch
- reframe / challenged assumption
- identity or situation callout
- proof / sourced evidence
- story / confession / discovery moment
- explainer / why
- how-to / instructional promise
- objection handling
- risk reversal / guarantee
- offer-led opening
- urgency or stakes when the constraint is real

A named frame is useful only when it serves the selected angle and mechanic.

## Native voice guidance

A hook should sound natural for the intended audience and surface, but “native” is not a license to fabricate authenticity.

Use voice patterns as structural inspiration, for example:

- candid admission
- discovery or realization
- conversational question
- shared frustration
- compact hot take
- specific situation callout
- progress story
- casual heads-up
- category observation

Do not copy a third party's distinctive line or present a model-generated sentence as something a real customer said.

## Awareness and belief fit

Treat awareness-stage guidance as a hypothesis, not a rigid funnel rule.

- Earlier-awareness audiences often need recognizable situations, useful curiosity, education, or desire before product detail.
- Solution- and product-aware audiences often need differentiation, proof, objection handling, mechanism clarity, or comparison.
- Most-aware audiences can often tolerate direct offer, risk reversal, or real urgency with less setup.

Account evidence and the specific buying situation override these defaults.

## Rules

- Do not optimize hooks for shock independently of message-market fit.
- Do not fabricate social proof, customer quotations, expert authority, statistics, urgency, scarcity, guarantees, competitor behavior, or product outcomes.
- “Sounds organic” is a style judgment, not proof that a hook will perform.
- A high click-through or thumb-stop rate alone does not make the hook a business winner.
- A winning hook on one angle does not make the same wording proven for another angle.
- If several hooks merely paraphrase one another, consolidate them instead of counting them as distinct learning cells.
- Platform-native details remain owned by the relevant channel skill when current platform behavior matters.

## Output

For each hook candidate, record as needed:

- angle
- mechanic
- hook objective
- frame / tactic
- primary psychological mechanism
- spoken / visual / overlay or static execution
- proof dependency
- controlled variable
- evidence or assumption state

The user-facing output does not need to expose tactic names or psychological labels unless they help the decision.
