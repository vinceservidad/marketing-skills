# Visual Format Selection

Use this reference after the angle, belief shift, and primary creative mechanic are clear. A visual format is the production vessel used to deliver the concept. It does not supply the strategy, claim, proof, or mechanic by itself.

Classify this as a technique and pattern catalog under [`KNOWLEDGE-TAXONOMY.md`](../_shared/KNOWLEDGE-TAXONOMY.md). Format-fit guidance is a planning hypothesis unless supported by account-specific evidence.

## Selection sequence

Choose format from the job the creative needs to do, in this order:

1. **Medium and placement** — static, video, carousel, feed, story, short-form, long-form, or other placement constraint.
2. **Mechanic requirements** — what must be shown, heard, compared, demonstrated, revealed, or inferred?
3. **Proof requirements** — does the claim need a demonstration, sourced number, customer evidence, expert explanation, product detail, or comparison?
4. **Audience state** — what is the best-supported awareness and belief state, and how much context is required?
5. **Product characteristics** — is the value visible, sensory, technical, experiential, identity-led, or difficult to demonstrate?
6. **Brand and compliance constraints** — what visual claims, depictions, comparisons, or tones are permitted?
7. **Production capacity** — what can be produced accurately and repeatedly at the required quality?
8. **Learning value** — can the selected format support an interpretable test rather than changing several strategic dimensions at once?
9. **Platform validation** — route current platform-native fit, placement rules, and policy-sensitive execution to the owning channel skill.

## Static format families

- **Product hero** — product-led composition with one dominant proposition.
- **Feature / benefit callout** — product image with mapped facts, features, or benefits.
- **Review / testimonial** — real customer evidence presented with traceable source and appropriate claim limits.
- **List / structured proof** — numbered reasons, benefits, objections, or proof points.
- **Comparison** — side-by-side alternatives, methods, states, or specifications where the comparison is fair and supportable.
- **Data / case evidence** — charts, measured outcomes, study findings, or case evidence with source and scope visible.
- **Native text + photo** — casual image or creator/customer-style visual with a short text hierarchy.
- **Editorial / note / letter** — text-led composition designed to feel like an editorial note, statement, letter, or personal observation.
- **Grid / carousel / sequence** — multiple cells or cards used when the message genuinely requires several distinct points.
- **Before / after or state contrast** — two states shown together when the transformation is genuine, policy-compatible, and not misleading.
- **Cultural / humor format** — meme, familiar visual grammar, or cultural reference where the reference is current and brand-appropriate.

## Video format families

- **Demonstration** — product use or performance shown directly.
- **Talking head / creator explanation** — one person explains, reacts, demonstrates, or narrates.
- **Founder / expert explanation** — a genuine qualified speaker provides origin, mechanism, or category context.
- **How-to / tutorial** — step-by-step instruction with the product naturally involved.
- **Customer story / testimonial** — genuine first-person experience with source and claim boundaries preserved.
- **Comment / question response** — a real or clearly simulated question becomes the entry point to the answer.
- **Interview / conversation** — dialogue, podcast-style discussion, or Q&A structure.
- **Lifestyle / cinematic sequence** — visual world, ritual, identity, or use context carries much of the message.
- **POV / narrative scene** — a scenario or story is enacted from a specific perspective.
- **Screen / interface walkthrough** — site, app, search, dashboard, message, or other interface is used as evidence or context.
- **Sensory / ASMR** — sound, texture, motion, or product interaction is central to the appeal.
- **Animation / generated visualization** — motion or imagery is used to explain something difficult to film; generated visuals must not be presented as real proof.
- **One-shot / minimal edit** — one continuous visual moment carries the message with minimal production complexity.

## Fit rules

- Do not assign a format to TOF, MOF, or BOF as a universal truth. Awareness-stage fit depends on the specific angle, mechanic, proof, offer, and execution.
- Do not call a format a winner because it worked for another brand, category, platform, or historical period.
- The same format can carry different mechanics and messages; the same mechanic can travel across formats.
- Choose the fewest format candidates needed to answer the test question. A large format library is a reference, not a production quota.
- If a visual itself implies a claim, comparison, transformation, endorsement, or product result, hold it to the same substantiation standard as written copy.
- Generated or simulated scenes are production assets, not evidence of real customer outcomes.

## Output

For each selected concept, record:

- medium and placement
- primary mechanic
- chosen visual format
- why the format serves the mechanic
- required visual proof or product behavior
- platform/compliance checks still required
- production dependencies
- adaptation plan
- format-fit confidence and evidence source

Format selection should make the strategic idea clearer, not replace it.
